Rank functions

Over a field or division ring

\(\mathrm{rk}(A) = \dim(\operatorname{im}(A))\)

  • Row/column operations don’t change the rank.
  • Matrices have unique row/column echelon forms.

Inner rank

Definition

\(\mathrm{irk}(A)\) = smallest \(k\) for which \[A_{n\times m} = B_{n\times k} C_{k\times m}\]

Properties

  • Equals rank over fields
  • Defined over any ring

Inner rank: annoyances

Depends on the ring

Over \(\mathbb{Q}[x^2,x^3]\) the matrix \[\begin{pmatrix} x^2 & x^3 \\ x^3 & x^4\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}x^2 \\ x^3\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1 & x\end{pmatrix}\] has inner rank \(2\)

Maximal rank doesn’t imply invertible

Over \(\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]\): \[\begin{pmatrix}0 & z & -y\\ -z & 0 & x\\ y & -x & 0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \\ z\end{pmatrix} = 0\]

Sylvester rank functions

Definition

\(\mathrm{rk}\colon \operatorname{Mat}(R) \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\) is a Sylvester rank function if:

  • \(\mathrm{rk}(0) = 0\), \(\mathrm{rk}(1) = 1\)
  • \(\mathrm{rk}(AB) \leq \min\{\mathrm{rk}(A)\,, \mathrm{rk}(B)\}\)
  • \(\mathrm{rk}\begin{pmatrix}A & 0\\0 & B\end{pmatrix} = \mathrm{rk}(A) + \mathrm{rk}(B)\)
  • \(\mathrm{rk}\begin{pmatrix} A & C\\ 0 & B\end{pmatrix} \geq \mathrm{rk}(A) + \mathrm{rk}(B)\)

Sylvester rank functions: examples

  • \(\mathbb{P}(R)\) = Sylvester rank functions on \(R\)
  • \(\mathbb{P}(R)\) can be empty
  • Over a field/division ring \(\mathbb{F}\), we have \(\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}) = \{\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{F}}\}\)
  • For \(\varphi\colon R \to S\) and \(\mathrm{rk} \in \mathbb{P}(S)\), \(\varphi^\#\mathrm{rk} \in \mathbb{P}(R)\): \[\varphi^\#\mathrm{rk}(A) = \mathrm{rk}(\varphi(A))\,.\]
  • Over \(\mathbb{Z}\): \(\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{Q}}\), \(\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{F}_p}\), convex combinations, …
  • \(\mathrm{irk}_R \not\in \mathbb{P}(R)\) in general!

Sylvester rank functions: operator rings

  • Over a field/division ring \(\mathbb{F}\): \[\mathbb{P}(\operatorname{Mat}_n(\mathbb{F})) = \left\{\frac{\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{F}}}{n}\right\}\,.\]
  • Group rep. \(\varphi\colon G \to \mathsf{GL}_n(\mathbb{F})\) induces \(\varphi\colon \mathbb{F}[G] \to \operatorname{Mat}_n(\mathbb{F})\) \[\mathrm{rk}_\varphi = \varphi^\#\frac{\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{F}}}{n}\]
  • Von Neumann dimension \(\mathrm{rk}_\Gamma \in \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma])\) for \(\Gamma\) a f.g. group coming from the unitary rep. \(\Gamma \curvearrowright \ell^2(\Gamma)\)

Sylvester rank functions: dimension

Definition

For \(A \in \operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}(R)\) and \(\mathrm{rk} \in \mathbb{P}(R)\), let \(M_A = R^n / AR^m\). Define \[\dim M_A = n - \mathrm{rk}(A)\]

  • Is an invariant of f.p. right \(R\)-modules.
  • \(\mathbb{P}(R)\) is in bijection with normalized dimension functions on f.p. right \(R\)-modules that are subadditive on short exact sequences and additive on direct sums.

Sylvester rank functions: integrality

Theorem. (Malcolmson, 1980)

If \(\mathrm{rk} \in \mathbb{P}(R)\) is integer valued, \[\mathrm{rk} = \varphi^\#\mathrm{rk}_{\mathcal{D}}\] for some unique division ring \(\mathcal{D}\) and some ring epimorphism \(\varphi\colon R \to \mathcal{D}\).

Broad questions

  1. When \(\mathrm{irk}_R \in \mathbb{P}(R)\)?
  2. Canonical/universal/largest element of \(\mathbb{P}(R)\)?
  3. Approximations inside \(\mathbb{P}(R)\): \[\lim_{i \to \infty} \mathrm{rk}_i = \mathrm{rk}\]

Profinite rings &
Sylvester domains

Sylvester domains I

Definition

\(R\) is a Sylvester domain if \(\mathrm{irk}_R \in \mathbb{P}(R)\)

Properties

  • Exists a division ring \(\mathcal{D}\) and \(\varphi\colon R \to \mathcal{D}\) such that \[\mathrm{irk}_R = \varphi^\#\mathrm{rk}_{\mathcal{D}}\]
  • Sylvester domains are domains of weak global dimension at most \(2\) (Dicks-Sontag, 1978)

Sylvester domains II

Examples

  • Fields and division rings.
  • \(\mathbb{Z}\), PIDs
  • \(\mathbb{F}[x,y]\) (Cohn, 1971)
  • Free algebras: \(\mathbb{F}\langle x,y\rangle\) (Cohn, 1971)
  • \(\mathbb{F}[F]\) for \(F\) a free group (Cohn, 1971)

Non-examples

  • Any ring with zero divisors, \(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\).
  • \(\mathbb{F}[x,y,z]\).

Sylvester domains III

Theorem. (Jaikin, 2021)

Let \(\varphi\colon R \to \mathcal{D}\) be a map to a division ring. Then \(\varphi^\#\mathrm{rk}_{\mathcal{D}} = \mathrm{irk}_R\) iff \(\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}) = 0\) and for any f.g. \(R\)-submodule \(M \leq \mathcal{D}\) and short exact sequence \[0 \to I \to R^n \to M \to 0\] we have \(I = \varinjlim R^k\) for \(k = n-\dim M\).

Profinite rings

Definition

A profinite ring is a compact Hausdorff ring. Equivalently, a profinite ring is an inverse limit of finite discrete rings.

Properties

  • Examples: \(\mathbb{Z}_p\), \(R[\![t]\!]\), \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!] = \varprojlim \mathbb{F}_p[G_i]\) for \(G\) a profinite group
  • No zero divisors implies its a local pro-\(p\) ring.

Profinite Sylvester domains: examples

Theorem. (Cohn, 1971)

Projective-free weakly-hereditary rings are Sylvester domains.

Corollary.

If \(G\) is a free pro-\(p\) group, \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]\) is a Sylvester domain.

Further candidates

Theorem. (S.-Jaikin, 2024)

Let \(G\) be a f.g. profinite group. If \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]\) is a Sylvester domain, then \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]\) is coherent, \(G\) is f.p. pro-\(p\) and \(\operatorname{cd}(G) \leq 2\).

  • \(\mathbb{Z}_p[\![F]\!]\) for \(F\) free pro-\(p\)?
  • \(R\) coherent but non-Noetherian profinite commutative local rings with \(\operatorname{gl.dim}(R) \leq 2\)?
  • \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]\) for \(G\) a f.p. pro-\(p\) group with \(\operatorname{cd}(G) \leq 2\)?

Commensurability

Theorem. (S.-Jaikin, 2024)

Let \(G\) be a torsion-free f.g. pro-\(p\) group. Then \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]\) is a Sylvester domain iff \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![U]\!]\) is a Sylvester domain for some (any) open subgroup \(U \leq G\).

  • False for abstract groups.
  • Neither implication is immediately clear.
    • \(\implies\) How \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]}\) behaves as a \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![U]\!]\)-module?
    • \(\impliedby\) How to build \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]}\) from \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![U]\!]}\)?

Strategy

  • \(\implies\) We must show \[\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]}(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]} \otimes_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![U]\!]} \mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!],\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]}) = 0\]
  • \(\impliedby\) A cadidate for \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]}\) is \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![U]\!]} * G/U\).
  • Given \(M \leq_{f.g.} \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![U]\!]} * G/U\) and \[0 \to I \to \mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]^n \to M \to 0\] we want \(I = \mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]^k\) for \(k = n-\dim M\).
  • \(I\) is free iff \(\mathrm{Tor}_1^{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]}(I,\mathbb{F}_p) = \mathrm{Tor}_2^{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]}(M,\mathbb{F}_p)=0\).

Free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\) pro-\(p\) groups

Free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\) I

Definition

\(G\) is free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\) if \(G = F \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_p\) for \(F\) a free pro-\(p\) group

Examples

  • \(\widehat{\pi_1(\Sigma)}_p\) for \(\Sigma\) a closed orientable surface.
  • Poincaré duality groups of dimension 2.
  • \(\langle x,y,w,z \mid [x,y]^p = [w,z]^p\rangle\) is virtually free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\).

Free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\) II

Properties

  • \(G\) is f.g. \(\implies\) \(G\) is f.p., any subgroup is again free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\).
  • \(\mathrm{cd}(G) \leq 2\) and \(G\) is coherent.
  • \(G\) is f.g. free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\) iff \(G\) admits a flag presentation: \[G = \langle x_1,\ldots,x_n,t \mid \mathrm{ad}(t)^{k_i}(x_i) = w_i, i=1,\ldots,n\rangle\] for some \(k_i \geq 1\),\(w_i \in F^p[F,F]\).
  • \(G\) is virtually mild (\(k_i = 1\) \(\implies\) mild flag presentation).

Free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\) III

  • \(R = \mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!] = \mathbb{F}_p[\![F]\!][\![t-1;\sigma,\delta]\!]\)
  • Filtered complete ring under the \(J(R)\)-adic filtration.

Proposition.

Assume \(G\) has a mild flag presentation. Under the \(J(R)\)-adic filtration, \[\mathrm{Gr}(R) = \mathbb{F}_p\langle y_1,\ldots,y_n,\ldots\rangle [\overline{t-1};\operatorname{id},\overline{\delta}]\] where \(\mathbb{F}_p\langle y_1,\ldots,y_n,\ldots\rangle\) is the free associative algebra.

The main theorem

Theorem. (S.-Jaikin, 2024)

Let \(G\) be a torsion-free finitely generated virtually free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\) pro-\(p\) group. Then \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]\) is a Sylvester domain.

  • Reduce to \(G\) with a mild flag presentation.
  • Explicitly build an embedding \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!] \hookrightarrow \mathcal{Q}\) into a division ring.
  • Apply Jaikin’s criterion to show \(\mathrm{irk}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]} = \varphi^\#\mathrm{rk}_{\mathcal{Q}}\).

Building the division ring

\(G\) is a mild flag pro-\(p\) group, \(G\simeq F \rtimes \mathbb{Z}_p\), \(F = F(X)\), \(R = \mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!] = \mathbb{F}_p[\![F]\!][\![t-1;\sigma,\delta]\!]\).

  • Embed \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![F]\!]\) inside \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p\langle X\rangle}[\![y]\!]\)
  • Extend \(\sigma\) and \(\delta\) to \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p\langle X\rangle}[\![y]\!]\) and build \[\mathbb{D} = \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p\langle X\rangle}[\![y]\!][\![t-1;\sigma,\delta]\!]\]
  • \(\mathbb{D}\) is a Noetherian domain, so embeds in a flat division ring \(\mathcal{Q}\).

The homological criterion

\(G\) is a mild flag pro-\(p\) group, \(R = \mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]\), \(\mathbb{D}= \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p\langle X\rangle}[\![y]\!][\![t-1;\sigma,\delta]\!]\).

  • Suffices to prove \(\mathrm{Tor}_1^R(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D}) = 0\) and consider submodules \(M \leq_{f.g.} \mathbb{D}\)
  • \(\operatorname{Gr}(R \to \mathbb{D})\) is a map \[\mathbb{F}_p\langle X\rangle[\overline{t-1};\operatorname{id},\overline{\delta}] \to \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{F}_p\langle X\rangle}[y][\overline{t-1};\operatorname{id},\overline{\delta}]\]
  • We can prove Jaikin’s conditions on the graded level
  • Lift infomation about \(\mathrm{Tor}_i^R\) from \(\mathrm{Tor}_i^{\operatorname{Gr}(R)}\)

Bonus: approximation I

  • If \(G\) is a finite group, the regular representation \(G \to \mathsf{GL}_{|G|}(\mathbb{F}_p)\) yields \[\mathrm{rk}_G = \frac{\mathrm{rk}_{\mathbb{F}_p}}{|G|} \in \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_p[G])\]
  • If \(G = \varprojlim G_i\) is pro-\(p\) with each \(G_i\) finite, we have \[\mathrm{rk}_G = \lim_{i \to \infty} \mathrm{rk}_{G_i} \in \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!])\]
  • Does not depend on the choice of \(G_i\)’s.

Bonus: approximation II

Theorem. (S.-Jaikin, 2024)

Let \(G\) be a torsion-free f.g. virtually free-by-\(\mathbb{Z}_p\) pro-\(p\) group. Then \(\mathrm{irk}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]} = \mathrm{rk}_G\).

  • The division ring \(\mathcal{Q}\) admits a non-archimedean valuation such that \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!] \to \mathcal{Q}\) is continuous.

  • If \(G\) is a pro-\(p\) group and \(\mathcal{D}\) is a valuation division ring such that \(\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!] \to \mathcal{D}\) is continuous, then \(\varphi^\#\mathrm{rk}_{\mathcal{D}} \leq \mathrm{rk}_G\).

  • However, \(\mathrm{irk}\) is an upper bound for any rank function, so \(\mathrm{rk}_G \leq \mathrm{irk}_{\mathbb{F}_p[\![G]\!]}\).

Semisimple groups

Classification

A complex semisimple Lie group \(G\) is a quotient of the form \[G = \frac{G_1 \times \cdots \times G_n}{Z}\] where \(Z\) is a finite central subgroup and each \(G_i\) is a simple simply connected complex Lie group, i.e. one of the following:

  • \(\mathsf{SL}_n(\mathbb{C})\) for \(n \geq 2\)
  • \(\mathsf{Spin}_{k}(\mathbb{C})\) for \(k \geq 5\)
  • \(\mathsf{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})\) for \(n \geq 1\)
  • One of the five exceptional groups \(\mathsf{G}_2(\mathbb{C})\), \(\mathsf{F}_4(\mathbb{C})\), \(\mathsf{E}_6(\mathbb{C})\), \(\mathsf{E}_7(\mathbb{C})\), \(\mathsf{E}_8(\mathbb{C})\).

Representations

  • Irreducible rational representations of \(G\) are classified by their highest weight \(\lambda \in \mathfrak{h}^*\), where \(\mathfrak{h}\) is a Cartan subalgebra of \(\operatorname{Lie}(G)\).
  • For \(G = \mathsf{SL}_n(\mathbb{C})\), \(\lambda\) is an \((n-1)\)-tuple of integers \(\lambda_1\,,\ldots\,,\lambda_{n-1}\). \[V_{(k,0,\ldots,0)} = \{f \in \mathbb{C}[x_1,\ldots,x_n] \mid f\text{ homogeneous of degree }k\}\] \[(fA)(x_1,\ldots,x_n) = f\left(A\begin{pmatrix}x_1 \\ \vdots \\ x_n\end{pmatrix}\right)\,.\]

Main question

Question.

Let \(G\) be a semisimple complex Lie group and \(\Gamma\) a f.g. subgroup of \(G\). How does the cohomology groups \[H^i(\Gamma, V_\lambda)\] behave as \(\lambda\) varies over the weights of \(G\)?

Motivation

  • If \(\Gamma\) is a lattice, the geometry of the locally symmetric space \(\Gamma\backslash G/K\) is encoded in the cohomology groups \(H^i(\Gamma, V_\lambda)\).
  • If \(\Gamma\) is an arithmetic group, the cohomology groups \(H^i(\Gamma, V_\lambda)\) are related to automorphic forms.

Cohomology and rank functions

Let \(\Gamma\) be a group of type \(FP_\infty(\mathbb{C})\).

Proposition.

For any \(i \geq 0\), there exists matrices \(A\) and \(B\) over \(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\) and a constant \(C\) such that for any complex representation \(\varphi\colon \Gamma \to \mathsf{GL}(V)\) with associated rank function \(\mathrm{rk}_\varphi\), \[\dim_{\mathbb{C}} H^i(\Gamma, V) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V)\cdot(C+\mathrm{rk}_\varphi(A) - \mathrm{rk}_\varphi(B))\,.\]

How does \(\mathrm{rk}_\varphi\) for \(\varphi\colon \Gamma \to \mathsf{GL}(V_\lambda)\) behave as \(\lambda\) varies over the weights of \(G\)?

Approximation for \(\mathsf{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})\)

\(G = \prod_{i=1}^k \mathsf{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})/Z\), \(\Gamma \leq G\) f.g. torsion-free.

Theorem. (S.-Guerrero, 2025)

If \(\lambda = (\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)\) is a weight of \(G\) and \(\varphi_\lambda\colon \Gamma \to \mathsf{GL}(V_\lambda)\) is the associated representation, then \[\lim_{\min_i \lambda_i \to \infty} \mathrm{rk}_{\varphi_\lambda} = \mathrm{rk}_\Gamma\,.\]

Corollary.

If \(\Gamma\) is of type \(FP_\infty(\mathbb{C})\), then \[\lim_{\min_i \lambda_i \to \infty} \frac{\dim_{\mathbb{C}} H^i(\Gamma, V_\lambda)}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}} V_\lambda} = b_i^{(2)}(\Gamma)\,.\]

Outline of the proof.

  • Reduce the statement to the case \(G = \prod_{i=1}^k\mathsf{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})\) and \(\Gamma\) is Zariski-dense.
    • Lifting matrices in a way that doesn’t change their rank.
  • Reduce further to proving it over a finite index subgroup of \(\Gamma\).
    • The group von Neumann algebra \(\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\) is a factor, so \(|\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N}(\Gamma))| = 1\).
    • The same holds for the \(*\)-regular closure of \(\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]\) inside the algebra \(\mathcal{U}(\Gamma)\) of affiliated operators.
    • It means we just have to prove that if \(\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi_\lambda}\) converges over a finite index subgroup, it converges over \(\Gamma\).

From \(\mathbb{C}\) to \(\mathbb{Q}_p\)

Theorem. (Cassels, 1976)

If \(\Gamma\) is a f.g. subgroup of \(G\), there are infinitely many primes \(p\) such that \(\Gamma\) embeds in \[G(\mathbb{Z}_p) = \prod_{i=1}^k \mathsf{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)\,.\]

Isolating the finite index subgroup: let \[U_i = \ker(G(\mathbb{Z}_p) \to \prod_{i=1}^k \mathsf{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}/p^i\mathbb{Z}))\,.\] Take \(\Gamma' = \Gamma \cap U_1\).

Comparing ranks

Theorem. Lück approximation ~ (Jaikin, 2019)

Let \(S\) be a subring of \(\mathbb{C}\) that embeds in \(\mathbb{Z}_p\). We have \[\mathrm{rk}_{\Gamma'} = \lim_{i \to \infty} \mathrm{rk}_{\Gamma/(\Gamma \cap U_i)} = \mathrm{rk}_{U_1}\] as rank functions on \(S[\Gamma']\).

Theorem. (Harris, 1979)

Let \(p\) be an odd prime. Then, \(\mathrm{rk}_{U_1}\) is the only rank function on \(\mathbb{Z}_p[\![U_1]\!]\) that satisfies \(\mathrm{rk}_{U_1}(x) = 1\) for every non-zero \(x\).

Ranks on Iwasawa algebras I

Let \(U\) be a compact uniform semisimple \(p\)-adic Lie group, and \(\mathbb{Z}_p[\![U]\!]\) its Iwasawa algebra.

Conjecture. (S.-Guerrero, 2025)

If \(\lambda = (\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_k)\) is a weight of \(U\) and \(\varphi_\lambda\colon U \to \mathsf{GL}(V_\lambda)\) is the associated \(p\)-adic representation, then \[\lim_{\min_i \lambda_i \to \infty} \frac{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p} xV_\lambda}{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V_\lambda} = 1\] for any non-zero \(x \in \mathbb{Z}_p[\![U]\!]\).

Ranks on Iwasawa algebras II

Theorem. (Fu, 2022)

The conjecture holds for \(U \leq_o \prod_{i=1}^k \mathsf{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_p)\) and any odd prime \(p\).

  • Proof sketch: \(\mathbb{Z}_p[\![U]\!]\) embeds in a completion \(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}\) of the universal enveloping algebra of \(\mathfrak{g} = \operatorname{Lie}(U)\).
  • The action \(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})} \curvearrowright V_\lambda\) factors through a central quotient \(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}/I_\lambda\).

Ranks on Iwasawa algebras III

Theorem. (Ardakov-Wadsley, 2014)

For almost all primes \(p\), the composition \[\mathbb{Z}_p[\![U]\!] \to \widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})} \to \widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}/I_\lambda\] is injective.

  • Canonical form for elements of \(\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}/I_\lambda\) and explicit lower bound for \(\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p} xV_\lambda\) in terms of this form.
  • Asymptotically \(\dim_{\mathbb{Q}_p} V_\lambda\) as \(\min_i \lambda_i \to \infty\).
